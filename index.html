Signs your East Valley home needs a panel upgrade

You may need a panel upgrade if your breakers trip when the AC and kitchen run together, lights flicker under load, the panel cover feels warm, a home inspection flagged capacity or obsolete equipment, or you are planning a Level 2 EV charger, kitchen remodel, pool equipment, shop circuits, or an ADU. Many Queen Creek, Gilbert, San Tan Valley, Chandler, and Mesa homes still run on original capacity that fit the day they were built, not today's heat loads and add ons.

This is a diagnostic check, not a scare pitch. Next Phase Electric starts with an on site load check so you know whether a repair, a breaker change, or a full upgrade is the honest path.

What a panel upgrade includes

Load assessment

We look at your current amperage, available breaker spaces, and what you plan to add. Tell us about EV charging, HVAC, a remodel, a pool, or a shop. That list drives the size of the panel, not a one size fits all sales chart.

Right sized equipment

When load requires it, a common path is moving from 100 amp service to 200 amp service. We size to your home. We do not push a larger panel than the calculation supports.

Permits, utility coordination, and inspection

Panel upgrades need a municipal permit and inspection. Next Phase Electric pulls the permit and coordinates the inspection path so you are not left chasing the town. If the utility needs a meter or service change, we manage that handoff and build it into the timeline in your quote.

Labeling and testing

Before we finish, circuits are labeled clearly and the work is tested. You should leave knowing what each breaker does and what changed in the panel.

Panel upgrades for EV chargers, remodels, and Arizona heat loads

East Valley summers already ask a lot of a home electrical system: multiple HVAC runs, kitchen loads, and overnight charging on top. A Level 2 EV charger plus AC at the same time is a capacity question, not a guessing game. Sometimes the existing panel can carry it. Sometimes the honest first step is a panel upgrade, then the charger circuit.

We handle both. See our EV charger installation page for Level 2 details. Remodel circuits are covered on our remodeling page. If you also want whole home surge protection discussed with the upgrade, ask on the estimate visit.

How long it takes and what to expect the day of

Most residential panel upgrades take about one day of on site work. Utility coordination or a meter change can add time. Your written quote includes the expected outage window so you can plan fridge food, work from home, and medical devices before we shut power down.

Can a panel upgrade support an EV charger later?
Yes. We size and label with future load in mind when you share EV or remodel plans up front.
Can my panel support a Level 2 EV charger and AC at the same time?
Sometimes yes, sometimes no. It depends on amperage, available breaker spaces, and the rest of the home's load. A licensed electrician runs a load calculation before installing a charger.
